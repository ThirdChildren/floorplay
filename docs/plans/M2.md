## Goal

A catalog of furniture on the **palm** (hand menu, palm facing up, on both hands) from which you **grab** a piece with a pinch, carry it onto the miniature, **put it down** with snapping to the floor and to the walls, **rotate it in 90° steps** with the wrist, and see right away whether it is **valid** (green outline) or **not valid** (red outline: it collides with another piece or with a wall, or blocks a door). A piece released outside the miniature goes back to the catalog; "Undo" in the menu undoes the last action; an invalid piece stays in the miniature with the red outline **and a short label that explains why**. The **miniature can be moved** (two hands, and also **with one hand** by grabbing the base) within 0.30 m of the anchor, with "Recenter" in the menu. A **hint** with no long explanations reminds the user of the palm menu until it has been opened for the first time (the full onboarding is in M5). In addition, the debt of the `² · × …` glyphs in the panel font is closed (atlas extended from Inter, ASCII fallback if the spike does not succeed within 45 minutes), and a spike removes the controller models from the CDN. Everything is verifiable on the PC with repeatable scenarios, and the snapping/collision/rotation logic is isolated in `src/logic/` with tests.

**New for M2**

**D13 · Furniture pose convention.** `rotationDeg` ∈ {0, 90, 180, 270}, **clockwise seen from above** with the floor plan drawn with −z at the top (like `viewpoints[].yawDeg` in `docs/DATA_FORMATS.md`); 0 = front facing −z (toward the top of the floor plan, as `asset-curator` requires). `position` = center of the footprint `[x, z]` in floor plan meters; the piece stands on the ground (y = 0, model origin on the ground at the center). Footprint = `[w, d]` from the catalog for 0°/180°, `[d, w]` for 90°/270°. In Three.js: `object.rotation.y = −rotationDeg · π/180` (same convention as the walls). **Data note**: the `rotationDeg` values of `staging.scandinavian` are not consistent with this convention (chairs and wardrobe face the wrong side relative to the table/wall); they do not change the footprint, so they do not break the M2 tests, but they must be reviewed in M5 (see risk R9).

**D14 · Held piece and preview.** While it is in the hand, the piece model follows the hand in 3D; on the house floor the **preview outline** appears (flat outline of the footprint, 0.10 m real thickness) at the **snapped** pose where the piece would end up: green if valid, red if not valid. On release the piece goes to the outline. A piece that is **put down** keeps the **red** outline if not valid, **no outline** if valid (so the view is not cluttered). A piece that is not valid because of `overlaps-furniture`, `overlaps-wall` or `blocks-door` **can stay in the miniature** (S2.3 requires it: "move it → valid"). The reason `outside-house` (center outside every room) is instead **not placeable**: on release the piece goes back to the catalog (new) or is removed (already in the miniature). The `valid|invalid` state and the reasons are **derived** (recomputed by `evaluatePlacement`), not saved in the store. **User decision 2026-10-05: invalid pieces stay in the miniature with the red outline, and the reason must be readable** (short label, D27).

**D15 · Proximity grab with its own system.** `OneHandGrabbable`/`useHandPinchForGrab` are not used: `GrabSystem` moves the object on every frame (in conflict with the snapped preview and with the return to the catalog), works on "raycastable" meshes at 1:20 scale and, as with `TwoHandsGrabbable` in M1, does not leave the control that is needed. A **`pinch-input`** module is extracted from `miniature-gesture.ts` (per-hand pinch from `selectstart/selectend` + pinch point = position of the `gripSpace`; a single function `pinchPoint(hand, out)` to be replaced with the index fingertip if the headset requires it) together with a small **arbitration** machine (`pinch-claims`): every pinch is "claimed" by one owner only, with priority **menu > furniture > two hands > one-hand translation (`pan`, D28) > room**. Consequences: while holding a piece, the second pinch does NOT start the two-hand gesture (it is the "tap" that rotates, D19) **nor a translation**; a pinch that grabs a piece, a menu item or the base does **NOT** select the room (`RoomLabelSystem` consults `isFurnitureInteractionActive()` and `isPanActive()`, as it does today with `isMiniatureGestureActive()`). A `pan` in progress with one hand that receives the pinch of the other hand **switches to the two-hand gesture** (the higher-priority claim takes over the hand already occupied by the `pan`). **Grab radii** (meters in the world, to be tuned on the headset): menu item 0.05 m from its center; piece in the miniature = pinch point inside the footprint (in plan) enlarged by 0.015 m and less than 0.06 m in height from the model floor; the piece with the closest center wins. Discarded alternative: ray grab (`RayInteractable`): the ray from the hand is not as precise as proximity at this scale and cannot be reproduced in IWER without orienting the hand.

**D16 · Placement rules (all constants in `src/logic/placement-rules.ts`, in REAL floor plan meters).** `GRID = 0.05` (free rounding); `SNAP_DISTANCE = 0.30` (wall snapping: at scale 0.05 a hand error of 1 cm = 20 cm real, so the radius is wide); `OVERLAP_TOLERANCE_FURNITURE = 0.06` (penetration depth below which two pieces do not collide: chairs tucked under a table, as in the preset); `OVERLAP_TOLERANCE_WALL = 0.005` (numerical noise only: a flush-snapped piece does not collide); `DOOR_CLEARANCE = 0.30` (free zone in front of and behind a door, over the whole doorway: clear width of the opening × (wall thickness + 2 × 0.30)); **flat pieces** (`size[2] ≤ 0.02`, the rug) do not collide with other pieces nor block doors, but remain subject to the walls; `MAX_PIECES = 40` (performance ceiling: beyond it, the menu does not pick up more pieces and logs `furniture limit reached`). Reasons (`reasons`): `overlaps-furniture`, `overlaps-wall`, `blocks-door`, `outside-house`. Snapping: only to **axis-parallel** walls (A and B are; oblique walls do not snap but still collide); the footprint edge snaps to the **inner face** of the wall (line center ± thickness/2 on the side of the piece) if the distance is ≤ `SNAP_DISTANCE`, one axis at a time; then grid on the axes that were not snapped. Windows do not collide (furniture can stand under them), door-type openings are gaps in the walls. The room of a piece is the one that contains its center (`pointInPolygon`).

**D17 · Store (version 1, extended).** Added to `AppState`: `furniture: PlacedPiece[]` with `PlacedPiece = { id, catalogId, instance, x, z, rotationDeg, roomId }` (`id` = `furniture:<catalogId>#<n>`), `nextInstance: Record<catalogId, number>` (**monotonic**: an id is never reused within the same session, even after undo) and `history: FurnitureSnapshot[]` (last 20 states of `furniture`, for undo). Actions: `placeFurniture(catalogId, x, z, rotationDeg, roomId)`, `moveFurniture(id, x, z, rotationDeg, roomId)`, `removeFurniture(id)`, `undo()`, `setFurniture(pieces)` (preset: does not write history). Every successful release = 1 history entry. The id of a new held piece is `catalogId#nextInstance` (expected) and the counter only increases on `placeFurniture`; **only one piece is held at a time**. `deserialize` also accepts states without the new fields (empty defaults): nothing is saved to disk before M4, so the version stays 1. With `debug=1` the `[soglia:state]` log prints the history as `"historyLength":n`.

**D18 · Palm menu.** A hand "opens" the menu when the palm faces **up** (angle between the palm normal and +Y < 35°, closes beyond 55°, 0.25 s of persistence to avoid flicker) and is not pinching. **Decision of 2026-10-06 (rerun 2, F1)**: the pinch of the same hand, a held piece, a two-hand gesture, a `pan` and the 300 ms after release prevent ONLY the opening; they never close a menu that is already open (with a piece in hand the open menu stays, dimmed and not selectable). The only way to close it is the palm no longer raised (beyond 55°) for 0.25 s. The menu panels are not disposed until their UIKit document is loaded (`src/ui/panel-lifecycle.ts`): no `[PanelUISystem] Failed to load panel` error. The palm normal is derived from the orientation of the `gripSpace` with a configurable local axis (`PALM_NORMAL_LOCAL`) **to be verified** in T2.11 (spike: semantics of the hand `gripSpace` in IWER and in the Quest Browser; alternative on the headset: `wrist` + `index/pinky-finger-metacarpal` joints from `XRHand`, only if `inputSource.hand` exists). It works on both hands; if both are raised, the first one wins. The `ui:palm-menu` entity **exists only while the menu is open** (created/disposed with `dispose()`, like the ghost hands). The items are **separate** entities (not children), with world position read from `ecs_query_entity` (**update of 2026-10-06, rerun 2 W1**: the menu is drawn as ONE SINGLE `ui:palm-menu` panel with title, six tabs and a bar of four buttons, updated in place on page change; the items are lightweight anchors with no rendering and no `PanelUI`, with the same `StableId` and the same position as before; measured cost of the open menu +9 draw calls per view instead of +28): `ui:menu-item-<catalogId>` (6 per page, in catalog order: 14 pieces = 3 pages), `ui:menu-undo`, `ui:menu-page-prev`, `ui:menu-page-next`, **`ui:menu-recenter`** (D28; always present in the menu bar, on every page). The bar buttons have **icon + text label** (D25). Panel at ≤ 0.6 m from the head (rule 8), 0.10 m above the palm, facing the head, text ≥ 2.4 cm (like the label). **M5 compatibility (one hand)**: the panel is built as a reusable component with mode `palm` | `pinned` (anchored in space), so M5 can anchor it without rewriting it. If palm-up is not reliable on the headset: plan B = small `ui:menu-toggle` button on the edge of the base (see R1).

**D19 · Rotation.** While I hold a piece, the wrist twist (swing-twist) around +Y relative to the start of the pinch, quantized in 90° steps with hysteresis (moves to the next step beyond 50°, goes back below 40°), rotates the piece; alternatively, **a second pinch of the other hand while the first one holds** rotates by +90° clockwise (robust, accessible, reproducible in IWER with an `xr_select`). Convention: the rotation is **relative to the one the piece had at grab time** and the piece turns like the hand: `rotationDeg = initial rotation − quantized twist` (the twist is positive counterclockwise seen from above, +Y). From 0°: hand counterclockwise by 90° → 270; hand clockwise by 90° → 90. From 90°: hand counterclockwise by 90° → 0. Pure logic in `src/logic/furniture-pose.ts`.

**D20 · Models and performance.** Every entry has a **procedural fallback block** (box `size` + dark front strip on the −z side, 2 draw calls, color from `palette.furniture`) created in code (ours, no asset): the app works without models and the tests do not depend on assets. The work of `asset-curator` (T2.8) replaces the fallback entry by entry with CC0 glTF. Constraints: **only CC0/public domain verified on the package page**, no Draco/KTX2 (`docs/RULES.md`), origin on the ground at the center, front toward −z, catalog dimensions ±2 cm, **≤ 1,500 triangles and ≤ 2 materials per piece** (M2 target: with the 14 preset pieces, ≤ 21,000 extra triangles per view; the `CLAUDE.md` budget stays ≤ 100 draw calls and ≤ 150k triangles), and **the app textures stay ≤ 4 in total and ≤ 1024 px**: the models must share **one single** texture (palette/colormap) or use vertex colors. If there is no suitable CC0 model for a piece (likely for `wardrobe`, `tv-stand`, `nightstand`) the fallback stays and it is declared.

**D28 · Moving the miniature: two hands and one hand (user decision 2026-10-05; T2.17 is mandatory).** Requirements: (1) it works **also with one hand only**; (2) it does not conflict with piece grabbing nor with room selection (D15: priority menu > furniture > two hands > `pan` > room); (3) the center of the miniature stays within **0.30 m of the anchor** horizontally, **y locked** at the anchor height; (4) there is a way to recenter it: `ui:menu-recenter` in the palm menu.
- **Two hands**: as proposed (pivot = midpoint of the hands, `center' = M_t + r · R(θ) · (center0 − M0)`, dead zone 5 mm; see the section "Moving the miniature").
- **One hand (`pan`)**: gesture = **pinch on the base** (the `table:plinth` disc, radius 0.45 m at scale 0.05) **outside every room and every piece**, and drag: the center follows the horizontal movement of the hand (`center' = center0 + (hand_t − hand0)`, y ignored), same 0.30 m limit and same 5 mm dead zone. **Starting zone (`isOnBase`, pure logic)**: pinch point within the base radius (scaled with the miniature), between −0.03 and +0.10 m relative to the top plane of the base (world units) and **farther than `PAN_GUARD` = 0.45 m real (in floor plan units, so 2.3 cm at scale 0.05 and 5.4 cm at 0.12) from the polygon of every room**: the guard covers the thickness of the exterior walls (0.125 m) and the grab margin of the pieces (D15), so **the three pinch zones are disjoint**: piece (footprint + margin) / room (`room:*` mesh) / free base (`pan`). The base scales together with the miniature (it is a child of `miniature:root`), so the free ring along the diagonal of house A is ≈ 2 m real (calculation: base radius 9 m, half diagonal of the bbox 6.58 m + guard 0.45 m): ≈ 10 cm at scale 0.05, ≈ 24 cm at 0.12. **Honest limit (R16)**: at high scale the ring may be **out of reach** (at 0.12 the near edge of the house is ~0.43 m from the center, i.e. at chest height, and the side ones ~0.7 m): the one-hand `pan` is meant for scales 0.05–0.08; at high scale two hands and Recenter remain. Values to be tuned on the headset.
- **Arbitration**: `pan` is an owner of `pinch-claims` with priority **above room and below two hands**; while a `pan` is active the **second pinch** (other hand) near the model **promotes** to the two-hand gesture (`pan upgrade`); it **does not start** if a piece is held (and grabbing a piece does not start during a `pan`: same rule as for two hands); menu items stay reachable (higher priority); a one-hand pinch on the base **with no movement** (< 5 mm) does nothing (no label, no selection).
- **Recenter**: item `ui:menu-recenter` ("Recenter", `LocateFixed` icon + text) in the menu bar together with Undo and pages; it runs `recenterMiniature()` → `offset = [0,0]`, `scale` = initial scale (0.05), `yawDeg` unchanged; log `miniature recentered scale=0.0500`; it **does not enter the undo history** (it is not furniture). If a `pan` or a gesture is active, Recenter closes it first.
- **Known limit (to be declared, not a bug)**: with **one hand only** the palm menu is not usable (one hand is needed to open and one to touch) until M5 brings the "anchored" mode (D18, `pinned`): so **one-hand Recenter belongs to M5**; in M2 the 0.30 m limit still guarantees that the miniature stays reachable and a `pan` brings it back.
- **Store**: `miniature.offset: [dx, dz]` (default `[0, 0]`, `deserialize` accepts states without it), actions `setMiniatureOffset(dx, dz)` (clamp in the logic) and `recenterMiniature()`.
- **Cost**: instead of 1.5 h it becomes T2.17a (1.25 h: pure logic + store + two hands) + T2.17b (1.5 h: `pan`, arbitration, Recenter).

## QA strategy for grabbing, releasing and rotating in IWER

Everything is done with **hands** (`xr_set_input_mode {mode:"hand"}`), never with controllers. Sequences already verified in M1 (memory `emulator-qa`) plus the new ones, which **must be confirmed on the first run** and corrected in the scenarios.

1. **Preparation** (as in S1.1): `dev-params.local.txt` → `browser_reload_page` → `xr_accept_session` → `xr_set_input_mode {mode:"hand"}` → wait 2 s. The head starts at (0; 1.6; 0) and O = (0; 1.35; −0.45) (not 0.95): read O from the last `miniature placed` line. Conversion plan → world with scale 0.05 and yaw 0: `W(px, pz) = (Ox + (px − 5.5)·0.05; Oy + h; Oz + (pz − 3.6)·0.05)` (5.5; 3.6 is the center of the floor plan of A). Hand height during transport: `h = 0.04`.
2. **Open the menu**: `xr_set_transform` on `hand-left` with position (−0.25; 1.15; −0.20) (below the plane of the miniature, 0.55 m from the head) **and `orientation` `Q_UP`** (palm up, fixed by the T2.11 spike); wait 0.6 s (persistence of 0.25 s + tool delay); `ecs_find_entities` with `namePattern: "^ui:menu-item-"`.
3. **Read the position of an item**: `ecs_query_entity` on `ui:menu-item-<id>` with `components: ["Transform"]` → position in the world (the items are not children of anything, D18).
4. **Grab**: `xr_set_transform` `hand-right` to the position of the item with `orientation {0,0,0,1}` (**always explicit**: `xr_set_transform` with the position only keeps the previous orientation and skews the wrist, M1 note); `xr_set_select_value` `hand-right` = 1; wait 0.3 s; log `furniture grabbed`.
5. **Carry**: `xr_animate_to` `hand-right` to position `P` (0.8 s); during the trip the piece follows the hand; halfway (or at the end of the run, with the hand still) `ecs_query_entity` on the piece with `components: ["Furniture"]` → `phase: held`, `status`, `outline` (check of the in-flight state).
6. **Release**: `xr_set_select_value` `hand-right` = 0; wait 0.5 s; log `furniture placed`; `ecs_query_entity` with `["Furniture", "Transform"]`.
7. **Grab a piece already in the miniature**: `hand-right` at the center of the piece (`W(x, z)` with `h = 0.04`), identity orientation, select 1, then as above.
8. **Rotate with the wrist**: with the grab active, three consecutive `xr_set_transform` of `hand-right` (same position) with yaw-only orientation at 30°, 60°, 90° (quaternion (0, ±sin(a/2), 0, cos(a/2)); **to be verified** whether `xr_animate_to` accepts `orientation`: if so, use it, 0.4 s per step), then release. **Rotate with the tap**: with the right hand holding, `xr_select {device:"hand-left", duration:0.2}`.
9. **Tolerances**: every final `x`/`z` is compared with ±0.01 m; if the grab does not start, repeat **only once** with the hand at ≤ 0.02 m from the center of the piece and report the offset (as in S1.2/S1.3).
10. **Control readings**: `[soglia:state]` (with `debug=1`) for the store; `ecs_find_entities` with `namePattern: "^furniture:"`; `browser_get_console_logs` with pattern `furniture|undo|menu|catalog` (and a small `count`: the buffer accumulates all loads, compare the timestamps with the `params source=` line).
11. **Cleanup**: release the pinches (select 0 on both), move the hands far away, `xr_end_session`, delete `dev-params.local.txt`.
12. **One hand only** (S2.9): to test the `pan` and the non-conflicts only `hand-right` is moved (the left one stays at rest with select 0 for the whole step, and the scenario checks it: no two-hand `gesture start`). The base is reached with the hand at (Ox + 0.40; Oy + 0.03; Oz) at scale 0.05 (ring beyond the house: house A measures 0.55 × 0.36, center in O, half width 0.275 + guard 0.023 → 0.30 m from the center; the disc reaches 0.45 m); **values to be tuned** with the real reading of the geometry in the spike of the first step of T2.17b.

## Moving the miniature (DECISION TAKEN: inside M2, mandatory; two hands **and one hand**, D28)

It started as the user's question "can I drag the miniature with two hands?"; on 2026-10-05 the user decided to do it **inside M2** with the **one-hand** version as well (D28, T2.17a/T2.17b, S2.9 mandatory at the gate). The sections below remain the rationale and the detail of the two-hand version; the one-hand part, the arbitration and the Recenter are in D28.

**Real state (before T2.17).** No gesture moves the miniature: `miniature-gesture.ts` sets uniform scale and rotation around Y; the position stays the anchor's (D3: 0.45 m in front, 0.25 m below the eyes) for the whole session. At scale 0.05 house A measures 0.55 × 0.36 m: the farthest point (the north corner) is ~0.69 m from the head (0.63 m ahead, 0.27 m to the side), at the limit of reach when seated but reachable; the rest is within ~0.6 m. At scale 0.08 it measures 0.88 × 0.58 m and the farthest corner is ~0.86 m away; at 0.12 it measures 1.32 × 0.86 m and the farthest edge is ~1.1 m from the head. **Zooming happens around the center of the miniature**, so the more I zoom in, the more the peripheral rooms **run away** from the hand. Today the only remedy is to rotate (yaw) to bring the room to the near side, and at high scale the near side ends up against the torso.

**Two-hand version (T2.17a).** In the same two-hand gesture of the miniature: the **midpoint of the hands** is the pivot (as on a map: you "grab" the house). With pivot `M` (at the start of the gesture `M0`), scale ratio `r` and rotation `θ`: `center' = M_t + r · R(θ) · (center0 − M0)`; if the hands start above the center, `center' = M_t` (pure drag). **Limits**: the center stays within **0.30 m** of the anchor horizontally (so part of the house is always in the reach volume and in the central area of the field of view, rule 8), **y locked** at the anchor height (no vertical drift, even if the hands rise), no tilt (as today: `tiltDeg` = 0), dead zone of 5 mm; the grab zone `withinReach` is no longer "within 0.35 m of the center" but "within the radius of the current miniature + 0.10 m and at ≤ 0.65 m from the head", otherwise at scale 0.12 you cannot start the gesture on the far rooms. A "Recenter" button in the menu (`ui:menu-recenter`) brings the miniature back to the anchor and to the initial scale. State: `miniature.offset: [dx, dz]` relative to the anchor (serializable, picked up again in M4).

**Consequences.**
- **`withinReach`** (`src/logic/two-hand.ts`): the signature changes (model radius); the tests and S1.2 stay valid because hands symmetric around the center do not move it (check 4: ≤ 0.02 m from O).
- **Onboarding**: no change (S1.6 is not touched; without text, the gesture is discovered by using it); a possible hint with open hands moving together is M5 polish.
- **Rule 8 (field of view)**: the 0.30 m limit and "Recenter" avoid pushing the miniature to the edges; the narrow field of view of the Meta VR Glasses must be tested on the headset (debt).
- **Rule 2 (no locomotion)**: it is not locomotion of the user, it is movement of the object; no teleport.
- **Arbitration** (D15, D28): with a piece in hand neither the two-hand gesture nor the `pan` starts, so the miniature is not "dragged" while furnishing; the pieces are children of `house:<id>` and follow the miniature.
- **Persistence/M4**: `offset` enters the store; at every session start the miniature is repositioned on the anchor (D3), so the offset is reset (like the scale, to be decided in M4).
- **M5 / T1.15 (one-hand zoom and rotation)**: one-hand **translation** is now in M2 (`pan` on the base). In M5 one-hand rotation and zoom must **not** reuse the pinch on the free base (it belongs to the `pan`): buttons on the edge of the base and/or the pinch dragged **on a room** are used (to be designed in M5 with the D15 arbitration). One-hand Recenter requires the anchored menu of M5 (D18).

**Cost/benefit.** Cost: ~2.75 agent hours in total (T2.17a 1.25 h + T2.17b 1.5 h; ≈ 1.2 h wall clock): pure extension of `two-hand.ts` with tests (pivot, clamp, locked y, coincident hands), `isOnBase` and `panStep` with tests, change to `miniature-gesture.ts`, new `pan` system, store field, menu button, a phase D in S1.2 and the scenario S2.9. Risk: the feel of "grabbing" with real hands (jerks, drift) and the reachability of the ring at high scale can only be checked on the headset; on the PC the logic is demonstrated with numbers. Benefit: at scale ≥ 0.08, where pose precision is better (1 cm of hand error = 12 cm real instead of 20), the far rooms stay within reach; indispensable for small furniture and for M3 `Measure` (which uses the same zoom); someone using one hand only can move the miniature (rule 9).
**Position in the order**: **after T2.13–T2.15 and T2.9b** (when the furnishing path works at scale 0.05 and the claims have been tested live), before T2.16 and T2.18. It is no longer cuttable: if time gets short, T2.8 is cut first and then T2.16.
